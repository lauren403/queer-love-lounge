The Lounge Method Deck · seventy five cards in seven suits

The stripe the flag lost meant sex.

Gilbert Baker’s 1978 flag had eight stripes. Hot pink sat at the top, and its word was sex. The six stripe flag most of us grew up under does not have it.

So this deck put it back, and gave it a suit.

Pull up a chair · Find the miss · Reach again

Most decks ask you to be interesting. This one assumes you are already tired.

Every card names the moment you would reach for it, asks you one question, and then shows you what an answer could sound like said out loud.

That last part is the bit nobody prints. Knowing what you feel is not the hard bit. Getting it across the room is.

These cards are for ordinary relationship conflict. They are not a tool for coercive control, intimidation, threats, stalking, sexual pressure or violence. If someone is using them to press you, put them down.

Pass is a whole answer. So is “I cannot find it in my body”, and so is not tonight. Nobody has to earn their way out of a card.

The seventh suit

The wanting

Twenty one cards on desire, refusal, sensory need, mismatch and repair. Written for bodies that do not all work the same way, and for people who were never handed a script.

It starts switched off. You turn it on when you want it, not the other way round. A no needs no reason, does not have to be earned back, and nobody deals this suit at anybody.

The other sixty seven

Not a game. A working library.

Nobody plays through seventy five cards. The full set is open online so you can go and find the one that fits the week you are actually having, read a whole suit at once, and take what is useful. It is free, there is no wall, and nothing is behind an email.

Pass, printed on the card

Say it, write it, or take it away

Not everyone thinks out loud. Write the answer instead, or take the card and bring it back tomorrow.

Bring it to a session

Keep the ones that landed. They are the fastest way into a first conversation with me, and they save you the summarising.

The overlap

You each mark a private list. It shows only what you both said yes to. What one of you wanted alone stays yours.

Ask me again

The deck remembers. A card that was too big tonight can come back in six months and find a different answer.

Or let it choose

Deal one blind, or have them pick three and you answer one. Their curiosity, your choice. Plus a two minute clock and a stop button that is always there.

Nothing is in play unless you put it there

You choose the suits before you start. No one has to remove a card in front of anybody.

Seventy five cards, built in my practice, for the couples I actually sit with. Most of what gets written about love assumes two people, a closed door, and a script everybody was handed. None of that held up in the room. So the deck was built the other way round: for relationships with more than two people in them, for people who are out in some rooms and not others, for bodies that do not all work the same way, and for couples who do not live in the same house.
